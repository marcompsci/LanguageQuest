## Market gaps (our inference from the above)

1. **A real story RPG for ages 7–13.** Kids' Spanish apps are clustered at ages 0–8 and built from flashcard mini-games. Droplets is the only one aimed at 7–16, and it teaches vocabulary only.
2. **One product from child to teen.** Teen options are general-audience or 13+ and not designed for kids.
3. **Motivation without punishment.** Energy and hearts, mistake counters, daily caps and streaks are the most-cited frustrations. → Language Quest: no lives, energy, timers or streaks. Wrong answers get a hint and a retry.
4. **Teaching in order.** Content is tested before it is taught, with little explanation. → Language Quest: can-do goals, guided before independent practice, an explanation after every answer, and a mastery check that encourages but never blocks.
5. **Speaking that works, and accessibility.** Reviews complain that speech recognition misgrades and that listening items can't be skipped. → Language Quest: speech checking is optional and on-device, with a no-microphone self-check; "Show the words" on every listening item; reduced motion; text alternatives.
6. **Honest family pricing.** Renewal confusion and hard cancellation show up for Studycat and Lingopie. → Apple billing only, Family Sharing, cancellation guidance, and the essentials free.
7. **Regional Spanish.** No competitor we found lets families pick Mexican, Spanish, Argentine, Colombian or Caribbean Spanish. → Language Quest has switchable variants, and answers in another region's Spanish count as correct, with a note.
8. **Transparent progress.** Parent views usually show time and points. → Language Quest separates activity from mastery, and understanding from production.

## Differentiators we must protect

- No ads, ever. No third-party analytics.
- No energy, hearts, streaks or loot boxes.
- Mastery is earned through spaced, delayed evidence, never XP.
- Original art and characters; we don't imitate competitors' branding, mascots or UI.
